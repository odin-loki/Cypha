## Draft: Winner v4: books, a bigger datastore, four experts

This round ran a review of the whole stack. Research and code-review agents
checked every stage, and a critic checked the plan; the confirmed fixes are
listed under *Upgrade round: review fixes* below. It then scaled the data the
model can draw on. The largest gain did not come from a new mixing method: it
came from giving the model book text.

**Evaluation.** The test slices are unchanged (enwik8 @96,000,000, Alice @20,000
and lcet10 @50,000, 16 KiB each), with a fourth added: PG-19 test book 9931 @100,000.
Every setting was chosen on separate tuning slices: enwik8 @97,000,000, Alice
@100,000, lcet10 @200,000 and PG-19 test book 33756 @100,000. PG-19 test books
are from the test split, which no training data comes from. Alice and lcet10
are Project Gutenberg texts, so book data puts them in domain. Every book used
for training was checked for overlap with the Canterbury files, and books with
overlap were dropped (below).

### More wiki in the ∞-gram datastore

The ∞-gram index is built at load time, so its corpus can be any text, not
only the 95 MB the shards were trained on. enwik9 is the first 10⁹ bytes of
the same 2006 Wikipedia dump as enwik8. Everything after its first 100 MB is
other articles, and the test slice is inside enwik8.

`cyphalm_trace` of the wiki test slice against the 300 MB datastore: 6.7% of
its bytes are in matches of 32 bytes or more, and the longest match is 133
bytes. Those are templates and boilerplate, not copies of the text.

| v3 winner, ∞-gram corpus (test slices) | wiki | Alice | lcet10 | private RAM | peak RSS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| enwik8[:95M] (v3) | 1.5927 | 1.9992 | 1.4984 | 0.9 GB | 1.4 GB |
| + enwik9[100M:305M] (300 MB) | 1.5719 | 1.9854 | 1.4928 | 2.3 GB | |
| + enwik9[100M:] (995 MB) | 1.5478 | | | 5.5 GB | 9.4 GB |

Each ×3 of wiki text gains about 0.02 bits/byte on wiki. The cost is RAM: the
index keeps about 3.6–3.9 bytes of suffix array per text byte, plus the text.
The index-memory fix below cuts both.

### Books

**Data** (`scripts/cyphalm_books.py`, id lists in `models/cyphalm_winner/`):
- **books_a**: the first 289 books of the PG-19 train listing, in name order, of at most 3 MB each (110 MB).
- **books_b**: a random sample, seed 19, of 720 more train books of at most 4 MB each (300 MB). Five were dropped, leaving 715 books (299 MB).
- Both samples exclude the Alice and Looking-Glass editions.
- Contamination check: every book was traced against alice29.txt + lcet10.txt with 64-byte matches. A book was dropped if it shared a span of 200 bytes or more, or more than 0.1% of its bytes.
- The script downloads the books from the public bucket and checks sha256 digests of each concatenation.

**Book shard** (`book_0`): a lean 8.6 MB shard trained on the first 8.6 MB of
books_a, with the same recipe as the wiki shards (upstream mixer settings,
occupancy fold 0.8, sentst_ dropped). `book_1` uses the same recipe on books_b.

**Book-aware experts.** Each 1-hour expert was continued with `byte_lm.py
train --init` on 100 MiB of interleaved 1 MiB blocks of wiki and books: LSTM
25 MB at learning rate 6e-4, Transformer 20 MB at 3e-4, each with 50 warmup
steps. The copies trade wiki for books. Keeping the originals and adding the
copies as two more experts keeps both, and costs about 1.5 ms a byte more.

**Format or content?** The Alice test slice was rewritten with LF line
endings, and again with its lines unwrapped. The gain from books then shrinks
from 0.248 to 0.193 bits/byte in both cases. So about 0.19 is content (the
language of 19th-century books) and about 0.055 is Gutenberg formatting (CRLF,
line wrapping at 70 columns).

| Alice test slice | v3 | v4a (c1, 2 experts) | gain |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| original (CRLF, wrapped) | 1.9992 | 1.7509 | 0.248 |
| LF line endings | 2.0184 | 1.8250 | 0.193 |
| unwrapped paragraphs | 1.9161 | 1.7235 | 0.193 |
