## 1. The project in one paragraph

Cypha is Odin's own C++ AI, and CyphaLM is its language model. Its aims are a working LLM-like system with **no neural networks at runtime**:
- symbolic and statistical machinery;
- online learning;
- low RAM;
- being picky about what it learns;
- saying "I don't know" rather than guessing.

CyphaLM does every task itself (PLAN rule 10): no string search or hand-written lookup may produce an answer.

Two tracks moved this week:
- **prediction**: bits per byte of `context8k`, the online content mixer;
- **writing**: generated text, judged by SCRIBE and by blind readers.

Answers (`qa`) did not change.

## 4. Writing: where it is

**The writer is K5** (`native/include/cypha/lm/word_kn.hpp`, `cyphalm_trie sample --k5 CLEAN.txt`):
- word-level Kneser–Ney of order 5 on 7 MB of clean enwik8 prose;
- nucleus 0.8;
- 256 exchange classes as the backoff floor;
- an 8-candidate sentence gate by word association with the prompt;
- a 12-word copy cap.

**Scores:**

| Judge | K5 | Human text |
|---|---:|---:|
| SCRIBE v2, GEN-A test | 0.524 | 0.92 |
| SCRIBE v3.1, GEN-A dev | 0.483 | 0.92 |
| Blind Claude raters, 1–10 (§13.31, §13.34, §13.36) | **about 2.1** | **8.4–9.0** |

**The key finding of the week: choosing among K5's sentences cannot fix K5.**
- K5 picks each word from the last four words, with no plan and no memory of what it has said. Its text is sentence-shaped but doesn't connect.
- Every chooser read at about 2/10: the gate, MBR, entity grid, LDA topic steering, paragraph MCMC, and a learned meta-mixer over ten structure experts.
- The structure mixer picks the *real* next sentence among 8 K5 drafts at 0.113 "sentence bits" (98%; K5 alone 0.415). It does so mostly because real sentences are *less* probable under K5, and among K5's own drafts that cue picks the oddest. Choosing the draft nearest the real sentence is at chance: K5's drafts hold no good sentence to choose.
- Every model blended into K5's next-word probabilities made the text blander: GLN, LSTM, fuzzy experts, online lambdas.
- More K5 training text helped plain K5 on SCRIBE v2 (test 0.380 vs 0.305), but not the upgraded K5, and SCRIBE v3 doesn't confirm either gain.
- **SCRIBE can be fooled by topic stuffing.** LDA rescaling raised v3.1 from 0.483 to 0.716 while raters marked it *down* (−0.22 at B 1.5, −0.42 at B 2). The raters are the deciding judge.

## 6. How writing is judged now: blind Claude raters

`scripts/blind_rate.py` (committed 2026-11-05) does it all:

```
python3 scripts/blind_rate.py make --out DIR --ids bench/blind/llm_rating/prompts.json \
    --arm K5=path/K5.gen.txt --arm NEW=path/NEW.gen.txt --mode pair --baseline K5 [--human]
# DIR/RATER_PROMPT.md holds four rater prompts; DIR/R1..R4.txt the packets; DIR/KEY.json the key (keep it from raters)
# send each prompt to a fresh subagent (general-purpose), save each JSON reply as DIR/R1.json .. R4.json
python3 scripts/blind_rate.py score --dir DIR --baseline K5
```

**How it works:**
- 50 GEN-A dev prompts (`bench/blind/llm_rating/prompts.json`), two raters per prompt.
- Each rater reads one packet file and nothing else, and never sees the key.
- Commit the packets and `key.b64` before the raters run, and register the rule and predictions in GENERATION_PLAN first.

**The rule:** an arm reads better if its paired difference (rate mode) or win share minus 0.5 (pair mode) is above 0, its 95% bootstrap interval excludes 0, and both rater pairs agree on the sign.

**Use `--mode pair`** ("which of two reads better") from now on: on the 1–10 scale every K5 variant got exactly 2 (§13.36).

## 8. What comes next: the new generator (GENERATION_PLAN §13.37)

Odin: "a Chain of Thought generating multiple sentences and let K5 gate it … skip the text form and go straight to combinations or permutations of the statistical encoding and gate them … process it before decoding". And: "Do all. … include all those experts from before."

**The steps** (search on IDs and codes; only the winner becomes text):
1. **Plan.**
   - Find the prompt's main entity and its facts in CyphaLM's fact store.
   - Build several plans as ordered tuples of fact IDs, and score orderings with the entity grid, transitions and topic.
   - Fallback when the entity has no facts: co-occurring entities and the structure relations expert's typed patterns.
2. **Skeleton.** For each planned fact, take sentence shapes over K5's 256 word classes with slots for the fact's subject and object, mined from real sentences that express the relation (F2's patterns and frames, or distant supervision over clean enwik9). Score them with the class 5-gram and rhythm.
3. **Fill.** A constrained beam with K5 on word IDs: the fact's words are fixed, K5 writes the rest. Keep the repeat ban and the 12-word copy cap.
4. **Gate.**
   - Every expert from before, joined by the structure meta-mixer: K5, class, topic, grid, relations, reference, time, transitions, rhythm, novelty, MBR.
   - **Don't train the mixer on "real vs K5 draft"**: that rewards odd text (§4). Rank fills of the real sentence's own skeleton and fact against others, or use the mix without k5 plus a K5 fluency floor.
   - Decode the winner. Write the paragraph sentence by sentence through the plan.

**Fact store:**
- **Today:** CyphaLM's own, from enwik8, in `bench/fact_store/` (`verify_f2r_aug/`: 25,392 seeds from infoboxes, tables and categories, plus 1,394 admitted prose facts; `patterns_raw.tsv`; README.md).
- Only about 30 of the 250 dev prompts name an entity with facts in it. Hence the store-growth session.
- Facts carry `byte_offset`, and `--plan-drop LO:HI` drops facts read from the prompts' own bytes.

**Judging:**
- SCRIBE v2 and v3.1, plus fact precision against the store, copy32 and coverage, on dev;
- then pairwise blind raters against today's K5 (§6), as the deciding judge;
- GEN-A test read once, only for an adopted arm.
